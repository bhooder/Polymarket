What these numbers mean

Expected if market is right: each price is the market's guess at the chance of winning (0.60 = 60%). If those guesses are right, this is what the bets should make on average: a small loss, the fees.

SD (standard deviation): how far luck alone usually moves the result. About 2 in 3 times the actual profit lands within 1 SD of expected; about 95% of the time within 2 SD.

Z: how many SDs the actual result is from the target. Above +2 happens by luck only about 2–3% of the time, so it hints the picks have a real edge. "Beat the market" compares with the expected loss from fees; "make money" compares with breaking even, which is a harder bar.

Be patient: with a real 10% edge it takes about 400 bets on average to reach 2 SD. Checking often and stopping the first time Z touches 2 makes a fluke much more likely, so the decision point is fixed at 200 settled bets. Group Z-scores in grey have under 30 bets and mean little.

Comparing strategies: both bet on the same picks feed, so they often share bets and their results move together. A gap between them only means something once both have many settled bets.

Strategies A, B and C use pretend money; Real bets use real money. Each bet goes to whichever US venue (Polymarket US, Kalshi or ProphetX) had the lowest price including fees, from the real order books. Bankroll = cash + open bets at cost. Research only, not financial advice.
